How it plays

The BLAST shot

Five clean returns in a row charge the meter. Once it turns gold, you have an instant to tap the screen at the exact moment the ball meets your paddle. Get the timing right and the ball comes back at a speed the PRONG can rarely reach. Lose the point and the charge resets — the risk is real.

Slow Zone

A capsule is launched from the middle of the court and it won't come to you: you have to intercept it with your paddle, leaving the ball for a moment. It slows the ball down while it's on your side. Lasts 8 seconds.

Paddle+

The other capsule makes your paddle bigger for the same 8 seconds. Both can be active at once — and the PRONG grabs them too, if you let it.

Three speeds

One button cycles through three levels. Each one speeds up the ball and the PRONG by the same amount, so turning it up is never a way to make things easier. First to 11 points with a 2-point lead wins — the official table tennis rule.

Android is coming

The port is already built and running.

Ping Prong was rewritten in Kotlin from scratch — not a single line shared with the iPhone version. Same court, same BLAST, same bonuses, same first-to-11-by-2 rule. It already runs at 60 frames per second, measured on a phone from 2019 — the same number as the iPhone.

What's left isn't code: it's the Google Play process, which requires a mandatory closed testing period before any new app can go live. That's why there's no date yet — when there is one, it shows up here.
